A clearer start

Morning Market Bot brings market news and a personal agenda together in a private Telegram message, with optional finance questions and follow-up conversations.

It runs on the operator's own computer or Raspberry Pi. This website explains the application and how it uses Google data; it does not collect credentials or connect accounts.

The morning brief

One message.
Three useful views.

  1. 01

    Today's calendar

    Event titles and times from the calendars the operator selects, including all-day events. Canceled events and invitations the user declined are excluded.

  2. 02

    Scheduled tasks

    Incomplete Google Tasks with a scheduled date, including future dates and past dates. The separate Google Tasks Deadline field and task times are not available through this connection.

  3. 03

    Market context

    A concise public-news briefing covering market moves, relevant holdings news and upcoming catalysts. Calendar and task contents stay out of market research and AI prompts.

Optional finance chat

Ask a question.
Explore the possibilities.

The private Telegram bot can research finance questions and compare market scenarios, catalysts and risks. Recent conversation context helps with follow-up questions; saved stock symbols can make answers more relevant.

Finance questions and a small recent conversation history are sent to OpenAI for research and answering. Google calendar and task records are excluded from this workflow. The chat does not place trades or provide an account balance view, and usage limits apply.

Use /help for commands, /sources for the last answer's references and /reset to clear conversation context. The scheduled morning brief remains a separate service.

Why Google access is requested

Read the agenda.
Leave it in your control.

The application requests read-only access to Google Calendar events and Google Tasks so it can add the requested agenda to the private Telegram brief.

It does not create, edit, complete or delete events or tasks. Google agenda data is read by the self-hosted worker, stored locally in a minimal snapshot and sent to the Telegram chat chosen by the operator.

Access is optional and can be revoked from the user's Google Account. Local delivery records and existing Telegram messages remain until the operator deletes them.
